# Unknowns and Prototypes

An unknown is acceptable when it is explicit and controlled.

For each unknown, record why it matters, whether it is discoverable, the latest resolution point, the responsible item or user decision, required evidence, and the consequence of remaining unresolved.

Use a discovery item when accessible investigation can answer the question. Use a prototype when the answer depends on observed behavior or feasibility. A prototype must have a narrow question, disposable or clearly bounded artifact, time or scope limit, observation method, success/failure signal, and follow-up decision.

Place discovery before dependent implementation. Do not let a prototype become production implementation without a plan revision, ownership, acceptance, and hardening criteria.
